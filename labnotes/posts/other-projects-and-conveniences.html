Other Projects & Conveniences

A quick note about a few other SimHub-related tools I have built alongside MCP4SH, including LCD-box and Final Drive Lap Board.

Category Experiments
Slug other-projects-and-conveniences
Updated 2026-07-20

MCP4SH is not the only SimHub-related thing I have been working on.

Some of my smaller tools are not trying to replace anything or become huge platforms. They are more about quality of life: making useful information easier to see, easier to manage, or easier to keep track of while driving and testing.

LCD-box

LCD-box is a SimHub Dash Studio dashboard focused on system information.

The idea is simple: while driving, testing, recording, streaming, or tuning a rig, it can be useful to see what the PC is doing without leaving the sim or opening extra monitoring windows.

Depending on the available SimHub data and system setup, it can be used to show things like:

  • CPU load
  • GPU load
  • memory use
  • temperatures
  • clock / performance information
  • general system status
  • other useful PC or runtime information exposed through SimHub

The goal is not to overload the screen with every possible number. The goal is a clear, compact dashboard for the practical information you may want nearby while testing or driving.

In that sense, LCD-box sits in the same general design family as my other tools: less hunting around, more useful information where you need it.

Final Drive Lap Board

Final Drive Lap Board is a SimHub-related lap board inspired by the idea of a Top Gear-style leaderboard.

It is meant as a simple way to keep track of laps, cars, tracks, drivers, attempts, and conditions in a more readable way.

The basic idea is: you drive laps, decide what counts, and keep a board.

It is not meant to be a strict esports timing system or a replacement for official leaderboard services. It is more of a personal/community lap board tool for testing, comparing, and having fun with different cars and tracks.

Final Drive Lap Board is built around things like:

  • track name
  • car name
  • player / driver name
  • lap times
  • lap attempts
  • custom rule sets
  • special conditions such as wet, fog, snow, or other notes
  • manual control over which laps are stored or counted
  • exportable data / CSV-style records

The manual side is intentional. Not every game exposes reliable invalid-lap detection, and not every session should be judged by the same rules. Sometimes you want a clean official-style board. Sometimes you want a custom challenge. Sometimes you just want to record a lap that was interesting.

Final Drive Lap Board gives you a way to do that without pretending every sim reports the same kind of timing data in the same way.

Shared idea

LCD-box, Final Drive Lap Board, and MCP4SH are different tools, but they come from a similar place.

They are all about taking something that can be messy, hidden, awkward, or overly technical and turning it into something more useful:

  • MCP4SH turns telemetry into clearer haptics.
  • LCD-box turns system data into a readable dashboard.
  • Final Drive Lap Board turns lap attempts into a usable board.

Different tools, same general philosophy: make the useful information easier to read, easier to use, and easier to act on.
