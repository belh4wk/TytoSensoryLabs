MCP4SH™ · String Theory Haptics for SimHub

High-level plugin overview

Haptics that aim to feel more like a weighted, connected chassis. Across games.

MCP4SH™ is a telemetry-first SimHub plugin built to make sim-racing haptics feel more connected, reduce masking, and make the information easier to interpret. The goal is clearer tyre scrub, road and suspension detail, drivetrain information, chassis/load cues, and less time spent rebuilding the feel every time you switch sim.

It is also the first commercial reference implementation of the wider MCP4H approach: preserve useful machine-state meaning, then adapt how that meaning is rendered to the receiver and hardware.

Yes, AI was used for drafting and faster iteration. The plugin's codec architecture was however entirely ideated, designed and constructed by me.

This project started as a thing I wanted to do because after getting new hardware and failing to back up my SimHub profiles, I didn't feel like searching for, or creating, a profile that felt right or believable for every game I play.

Ironically, a few hours of doing exactly that would've fixed my issue... but I digress; I figured, what if I take a different approach that allows me to just switch games whenever I like without having to second-guess if i'm feeling everything i'm expecting (or not)?

So I started building this, and as I progressed, I assumed others might have or find use for it, so I added some more useful features and decided to share.

Wut?

Sanitized telemetry feed

MCP4SH tries to make grip, scrub, suspension, road texture, drivetrain, and weight-transfer style cues easier to separate and interpret. It will not make you faster, but it might help you be more consistent.

How?

Less #brrrzt

The goal is to reduce noise in the vibration signals so the feedback feels more coherent and, more like a moving chassis. Near-constant buzzing and not really knowing where it's coming from or trying to tame it, almost gone.

And why should i care?

Seat. Of. Pants.

Reliable, consistent haptics helps you trust what you are feeling faster, with less second-guessing and less re-tuning each time you wanna switch games because you're not sure anymore. It helps you drive intuitively.

Why "String Theory"?

A driving-coaching idea about connected inputs.

In performance-driving instruction, "string theory" or the "string analogy" asks you to imagine the steering wheel and pedals connected by an invisible string. As steering input increases, brake or throttle input generally has to give something back; as the wheel opens, more longitudinal input becomes available. It is a memorable way to teach that steering, braking and acceleration are not independent on/off commands when the tyres are already using a finite grip budget.

MCP4SH borrows the relationship idea, not a literal steering-to-pedal formula. The plugin treats tyre, suspension, road, drivetrain and chassis information as parts of a coupled vehicle state, then translates the useful relationships into tactile cues that can coexist without simply piling more vibration on top. The driving analogy is deliberately simple; advanced coaching material also points out that the real relationship changes with vehicle balance, rotation, steering feedback, line and timing.

Free + Premium

Core haptics free. Premium for deeper control.

The free version is not a crippled demo. It includes the core MCP4SH String Theory Haptics experience and the Setup Assistant.

That means you can map the shakers on your rig, proof-pulse your sound outputs, create explicit shared-channel mappings where needed, generate the matching SimHub sound output profile, and get driving without first becoming a SimHub routing expert.

MCP4SH Premium is the current paid tier. The earlier Pioneer tier is retired; existing Pioneer users remain grandfathered.

Premium unlocks the extra control layer, including the Advanced controls and ST Tensioner presentation, for people who want to shape the balance and feel around their own rig rather than only use the supplied defaults.

The store listing remains the source of truth for current pricing, promotions and exactly what is included at the time you buy.

Why Premium?

Because the useful baseline should still be usable for free.

  • Free gets you driving: the core codec, supplied profile and Setup Assistant are enough to use MCP4SH as intended.
  • Premium gives you more control: Advanced tuning lets you change emphasis without dismantling the supported signal chain.
  • The product can keep moving: paid licenses directly support compatibility work, hardware testing, telemetry refinement and the boring release-engineering bits.
  • No profile-sharing dependency: the goal remains a coherent baseline that adapts across titles and rigs, not a library of game-specific profiles you have to keep hunting down.

Support baseline

Not a standard SimHub effects stack.

MCP4SH is built as a telemetry-to-haptics codec rather than a usual SimHub profile made from standalone raw formulas. The plugin processes available telemetry signals from the game before exposing shaped public outputs used by the supplied SimHub profile.

That means separated graphs, manually edited formulas, or heavily modified profiles can be useful for personal experimentation, but they are not the supported reference for accurately and methodically judging whether MCP4SH is behaving correctly. The supported baseline is the current supplied MCP4SH plugin, profile, generated channel map, and Setup Assistant workflow.

The frequency ranges, tactile carrier choices and vehicle-motion interpretation are not arbitrary lookup tables. They are informed by published work on tactile perception, vehicle vibration and transfer behaviour, then tested empirically against the telemetry and hardware MCP4SH actually has to work with. Research provides the engineering context; telemetry quality, calibration and the real rig still determine the implementation.

FAQ

But errrm...

What does it actually do?
It is a SimHub ShakeIt Bass Shakers haptics plugin that tries to make feedback feel more like a car than a bunch of vibrations fighting for attention and bandwidth. The goal is to better separate tyre, scrub, road, suspension, drivetrain, and weight-transfer style cues so the rig feels more informative and less chaotic, and more intuitive.
Is this just “more vibration”?
No. That is the opposite of the point. MCP4SH is meant to reduce the sense of random buzzing and make the overall signal easier to trust. More output is not automatically better output.
Do I need to spend ages tuning it per game?
Nope, pretty much works out of the box. That said, rigs, transducer layouts, amps, and personal taste obviously still matter. This plugin does not fix foundational issues with factors that impact designed-for feel. The feel is going to be similar, but emphasis of some effects may differ due to how the data coming from the game is shaped. That also means though, that different cars in a specific title also feel slightly different. The cleaner the telemetry from the game, the better this plugin will perform.
Does it work the same in every sim?
The intended semantics and overall balance are meant to stay recognizable across sims, but the source telemetry is not identical. MCP4SH conditions and normalizes what each title exposes so you do not start from scratch every time, while still allowing cars and titles to retain meaningful differences. Cleaner telemetry gives the codec more to work with.
Why are there not more videos yet?
Because haptics are awkward to show well, and the project reached a releasable state before the showcase material was finished. Workin' on it.
Will "this" be better than what I have now?
Since I do not know your current experience or level of satisfaction with what you have, I can not accurately answer that. What I can say is that this is intended to make it easier to set up per game, and that it is designed to feel more like a weighted, connected chassis. It is up to you to decide if I've accomplished that.
Will this work on my hardware?

If your current hardware is driven by SimHub ShakeIt Bass Shaker effects, then yes, it should.
However, and I can not stress this enough: Mounting discipline is crucial, not optional.

As I learned myself, all transducers on any given rig should be:
- mounted correctly: flush with its mounting surface or directionally in-line with/on top of the (virtual) axis along which the vibrations will travel.
- hard-mounted: nuts and bolts, heavy duty doublesided mounting tape or strong zip-ties.
- isolated/separated: use shock-absorbing washers or bushings to isolate each transducer (sounds counterintuitive, i know), this limits frame/rig cross-talk.

use this rule of thumb: if you can hear the shaker more than you feel it, it's not mounted right, or something on your rig is loose/rattling.

What hardware was this designed on/for?
Testing setup was:
    2x Nobsound NS-10G Pro amps
    2x Dayton BST-1 shakers (under pedal plate and seat)
    3x Dayton TT25-16 pucks (brake, backrest, handbrake/shifter)

Any claim made about the quality of this plugin's output, is based entirely on my own frame of reference, which is, based on market-research, represents a decent demographic of both beginners and enthusiasts.

Recent 1.1.13.x work also strengthened multichannel Windows audio endpoint detection and routing, including awkward 8-channel USB/SPDIF cases. If SimHub can expose a compatible endpoint, the Setup Assistant is now much better at identifying and proof-pulsing the intended physical channels.

It should work on anything that connects to SimHub to get its haptic effects via ShakeIt Bass Shakers.
Is this better than default SimHub effects?
No such claims are or will be made. Better or worse are both subjective terms.
That said, according to initial reviews and comments i've received, it is quicker to get going with something that's usable in most games. Tweaking for feel should be more intuitive.
How is it different then from default SimHub effects?
Effects in SimHub are mostly separated, the more you enable, the harder it becomes to keep them all making sense, especially when it matters. With MCP4SH, even with all effects enabled, you can still reliably tell the difference between what's firing, making it more intuitive to feel what the car is doing underneath you.
Can I manually separate or edit MCP4SH formulas?
You can experiment, but that is outside the supported baseline. MCP4SH is not a standard SimHub effects setup where every effect should be treated as a raw standalone formula. Public outputs are already shaped by the plugin's normalization, cleanup, gating, routing, and ST balancing/tensioning layers. Manual separation can therefore be misleading if it is used to judge the full intended behaviour.
Why is there a Premium version?
Because the useful baseline should remain accessible while the deeper control layer helps fund continued development. Premium unlocks the Advanced tuning layer for people who want more control over balance and emphasis without dismantling the supported signal chain.
What does MCP4SH Premium add?
Free gets the core codec, supplied profile and Setup Assistant working. Premium adds the deeper Advanced control layer, including the ST Tensioner presentation, and directly supports compatibility work, testing and continued development. Sanity/Reality check

Some disclaimers

This is not magic, instant pace, or one-click perfection (almost, but not quite). It is however a serious attempt to make haptics more coherent and useful, and plug and play (ish). Your rig, transducer layout, amp chain, gain staging, and sims still matter. The goal is to start from a stronger place and give you something more ready-to-use. It removes complexity in tuning without losing fidelity. Do not treat this as an "automagically fix my haptics" solution. If mounting discipline (see FAQ re. hardware) is meh, this will not fix that.
